THEORETICAL RESEARCH DRAFT

Human Consciousness, Perception and AI

A conceptual inquiry into how perception is constructed by the brain and how this question can inform careful discussion of artificial intelligence. The work draws on predictive-processing ideas and neuroscience as a theoretical lens, not as proof that human consciousness and AI are equivalent.

Theoretical research draft. No original experiments or empirical validation are claimed. Proposed future work would require precise hypotheses, a literature review and an appropriate experimental design.

← Back to Research Portfolio
01 — ABSTRACT

Overview

This theoretical draft examines perception as an active process in which the brain interprets sensory input in relation to prior expectations and context. It asks what such accounts can—and cannot—contribute to discussions of consciousness and AI. The framework is intended to clarify concepts and generate testable questions rather than present a settled theory of consciousness. It reports no original experimental data, and its claims require careful engagement with the scientific literature and empirical evaluation.

02 — RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions to investigate

  • How do predictive-processing accounts describe the relationship between sensory input, expectation and perception?
  • Which concepts of consciousness must be distinguished from perception and intelligent behavior?
  • What observations could help test specific claims rather than broad analogies?
  • How can AI comparisons be framed without assuming that similar outputs imply similar inner experience?

Keywords

consciousness; perception; predictive processing; neuroscience; artificial intelligence; philosophy of mind
