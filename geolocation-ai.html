WORKING MANUSCRIPT

AI-Assisted Geolocation Without GPS

This working manuscript explores the problem of estimating geographic location when GPS is unavailable or unreliable. It considers the broader role that alternative signals, contextual evidence and AI-assisted inference may play, while treating accuracy, uncertainty, environmental dependence and validation as central research questions.

01 — ABSTRACT

Abstract

Recent advancements in artificial intelligence have enabled the development of powerful geolocation systems capable of identifying or estimating the geographic location of individuals from ordinary images and videos, even in the absence of GPS metadata. These systems rely on a combination of computer vision, multimodal learning, optical character recognition (OCR), and large-scale visual data comparison to extract environmental and contextual cues such as architecture, vegetation, road structures, language, and lighting conditions.

While these technologies offer significant benefits in areas such as emergency response, disaster management, journalism verification, and open-source intelligence (OSINT), they simultaneously introduce serious concerns regarding privacy, personal safety, and potential misuse. The ability to infer precise locations from publicly shared media raises critical ethical questions about surveillance, consent, and digital rights.

This paper examines the dual-use nature of AI-powered geolocation technologies, highlighting both their societal value and associated risks. It further proposes a set of governance principles, including transparency, usage restrictions, and international regulatory frameworks to ensure responsible deployment. The goal is to balance technological innovation with the protection of individual privacy and fundamental human rights in the digital era.

02 — RESEARCH QUESTIONS

Questions to investigate

  • Which non-GPS signals or contextual sources may be available in the target environment?
  • How should conflicting evidence and uncertainty be represented?
  • How would performance be compared against a transparent baseline across different environments?
  • What privacy safeguards are needed when location is inferred from contextual data?
