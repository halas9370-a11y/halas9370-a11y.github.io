HALALOYA — PROJECT 02

NON-INVASIVE
BRAIN INTERFACE

Exploring a new approach to human-computer interaction.

A research concept exploring whether artificial intelligence, advanced sensing and signal processing can help interpret human intent without surgical brain implantation.

← Back to HalaLoya
01 — THE CONCEPT

The brain interface
without surgery.

The project explores non-invasive approaches to brain-computer interaction using external sensing technologies combined with artificial intelligence. The long-term research question is how brain-related signals could be captured, interpreted and translated into useful commands while preserving human safety, privacy and control.

01A — PROJECT VISUALS

Visual concepts for a non-invasive neural interface.

02 — RESEARCH DIRECTION

Signals into
meaning.

The research direction combines neuroscience, signal processing, machine learning and human-computer interaction.

01 — SENSING

Non-Invasive Signals

Exploring external methods of capturing brain-related activity, including EEG and other non-invasive sensing approaches.

02 — AI

Signal Interpretation

Using machine-learning approaches to identify patterns within complex and noisy biological signals.

03 — INTENT

Human Intent

Exploring how neural and physiological signals could potentially be translated into meaningful interaction commands.

04 — ADAPTATION

Adaptive Intelligence

Investigating AI systems that can adapt to individual users rather than relying on identical signal patterns for everyone.

05 — ACCESSIBILITY

Assistive Technology

Potential applications include communication and computer interaction for people with severe motor limitations.

06 — HUMAN CONTROL

Human-Centered Design

The system is envisioned around user control, safety, privacy and meaningful human oversight.

03 — POSSIBLE APPLICATIONS

Technology for
human capability.

The research is exploratory. Potential future applications could include assistive communication, hands-free computer interaction, rehabilitation technologies and accessibility tools.

04 — RESPONSIBLE NEUROTECHNOLOGY

Innovation requires
boundaries.

Brain-related technologies raise important questions about privacy, consent, safety and human autonomy. Responsible development is therefore treated as a fundamental part of the research direction.

Privacy
Protecting sensitive neural and personal information.
Consent
Keeping meaningful participation under user control.
Safety
Prioritizing non-invasive and safety-conscious research.
Human Autonomy
Technology should assist people, not replace their agency.
05 — DEVELOPMENT

From research question
to experiment.

Research Status

This is an evolving research concept. The project explores scientific and technological pathways toward non-invasive brain-computer interaction. Further experimentation, validation and interdisciplinary research are required before practical conclusions can be reached.

RESEARCH ROADMAP — PROPOSED

A cautious path from concept to evidence

The following steps are proposed future work, not completed milestones.

  1. Define one assistive task and measurable success criteria with relevant users and researchers.
  2. Review the evidence and choose an appropriate non-invasive sensing method, such as EEG, for that task.
  3. Establish a safe study protocol, informed consent, privacy protections and any required ethics approval before collecting human data.
  4. Evaluate baseline models on consented data using careful validation across sessions or participants.
  5. Measure reliability, false activations, usability and limitations before making claims about practical readiness.
06 — VISION

A different interface
between human and machine.

The long-term vision is to investigate whether intelligent, non-invasive interfaces can make technology more accessible to people while maintaining safety, privacy and human control.
